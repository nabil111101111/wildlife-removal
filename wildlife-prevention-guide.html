Wildlife Prevention: Keep Them Out Before They Move In

The cheapest wildlife job is the one that never happens. Prevention is a list of small, dull, permanent fixes: screen the vents, cap the chimney, seal the quarter-inch gaps, starve the yard of easy food. Do them in a weekend or book them with an operator, and the October entry push passes your house by.

The quarter-inch rule and the roofline pass

Hold one number in mind: a quarter of an inch. Mice pass a gap that size, young rats and bats need little more, and squirrels chew a small weak spot up to entry size in an afternoon. Prevention is a slow walk around the house with that number as your gauge. Soffit returns, ridge and gable vents, chimney flashing, plumbing vents, the line where a porch roof meets the wall, cable and pipe entries, garage door corners, and the foundation below them all. Mark every gap, soft board and loose screen on a list as you go. The list, not your memory, is the job.

Fixes match the point. Quarter-inch hardware cloth over vents, fixed with screws and washers, never staples alone. A metal chimney cap with mesh sides. Flashing and backer plus sealant on flat gaps, with foam used only as backing behind metal, never as the barrier. Replace rotten fascia rather than screening over it, because fixings need sound wood and animals test soft boards first. Photograph each fix. Your future self, your insurer and your next operator all work better from photographs than from a shrug.

  • Metal chimney cap fitted and checked yearly
  • Tree limbs cut back from the roof

Food, water and the yard's welcome mat

Animals den where food is close. Bins with locking or weighted lids, pet food fed indoors or cleared by dusk, bird feeders set away from the roofline and cleaned of spill, fallen fruit picked up, and grills scraped, together these lower your address on every animal's map of the street. Compost that includes kitchen scraps deserves a closed bin. None of this starves wildlife. It simply removes the bonus that made your garden the obvious base for a den under your deck or a roost over your bedroom.

Water counts too. A dripping outside tap, a clogged gutter holding a pool and a birdbath by the foundation all serve denning animals through dry spells. Fix the drip, clear the gutter, and you have also done your roof and foundation a favour. Prevention lists often stop at the roofline. The yard is the other half of the same decision an animal makes when it weighs your house against the empty one two doors down.

Decks, sheds and the annual check

Ground level needs its own pass. Skirt open deck sides or fit the buried mesh barrier with an L-footer before a den exists, because the barrier is a weekend job on an empty den and a longer one after eviction. Screen crawlspace vents with hardware cloth, keep firewood stacked away from the house wall, and check shed doors for the groundhog gap at the corner. Then set the habit that makes all of it stick: one walk each September with the list from last year, before the entry push, catching the lifted flashing and chewed screen while they are still small.

The September sealing pass, step by step

1. Walk the roofline list

Check chimney, vents, soffits, fascia and every roof joint against the quarter-inch rule and note each gap.

2. Walk the ground list

Check foundation entries, garage corners, crawlspace vents, deck skirts and shed doors.

3. Fix in material order

Fit metal first: caps, screens and flashing. Use sealant only as a finisher over backer or behind mesh.

4. Starve the yard

Secure bins and pet food, move feeders from the roofline, clear fallen fruit and fix drips.

5. Photograph and file

Record every fix with a photo and keep the list for next September's comparison walk.

Frequently asked questions about prevention

How do I keep wildlife out of my house for good?

Seal the structure to the quarter-inch standard, cap the chimney, screen every vent in metal, bury a mesh barrier at decks and porches, and remove the food welcome mat from the yard. Then repeat one walk each September. No single product does this. It is a condition of the house, maintained like gutters or a boiler service, and once reached it holds for years because animals test the outside and move on to softer houses. What is the quarter-inch rule in wildlife prevention?

It is the size standard for gaps. A mouse passes a quarter-inch gap, so any opening of that size or larger is treated as a door until sealed or screened in material finer than the animal. Bats need only a few millimetres more along a slot. Squirrels start smaller and chew up. The rule turns a vague resolution to seal the house into a gauge you can carry: if your little finger's nail fits the gap, it goes on the list. What is the best way to keep raccoons out of an attic?

Remove every invitation at once: limbs cut back so the roof is not a bridge, chimney capped, soffit and vent weak points sealed in metal, bins locked and pet food indoors. Raccoons test houses with their hands, and they remember the ones that paid. A raccoon that finds no entry and no food works the street and settles elsewhere within days. One strong fix, like a cap, beside one weak soffit is a puzzle the raccoon will solve. That approach keeps the work humane, avoids sealing an animal inside, and gives you a record of exactly what was done.

Do chimney caps and vent screens really prevent animals?

Yes, they close two of the most-used entries in New York housing. Uncapped chimneys admit raccoons, squirrels and chimney swifts every spring, and unscreened gable and bathroom vents are standard bat and wasp routes. The cap must be metal with mesh sides sized to the flue, and screens must be hardware cloth fixed with screws. Plastic caps and loose mesh fail the same winter they are fitted, which is worse than none because the failure is invisible from the ground. How do I stop animals getting in along the roofline?

Walk it like an animal: every joint is a candidate. Porch roof junctions, soffit returns, ridge vent ends and the shadow line behind gutters hide the gaps that matter. Seal flat joints with flashing, guard long vents with purpose-made covers backed by mesh, and replace soft fascia instead of covering it. Where height or pitch makes the walk unsafe, that portion is a fair job for an operator with ladders and a harness, priced as prevention rather than as an emergency later. Can tree branches really give animals roof access?

Yes. Squirrels jump several feet from a limb to a roof, and raccoons climb limbs that hang over or touch the structure and walk down. Cut limbs back so no branch reaches within a body's leap of the roofline, and clear the climbers, ivy and trellis, that give a raccoon a ladder up a wall. It is the cheapest item on this page and the one most often skipped, because the tree looks innocent from the garden in June. Should I book an annual wildlife inspection?

What food sources attract wildlife to a house?

Unsecured bins lead, then pet food left out, bird feeder spill, fallen fruit, open compost with kitchen scraps, and grills with fat in the tray. Water from drips and clogged gutters completes the offer. None of these need banning from your life. They need managing by dusk: lids locked, bowls in, spill swept, fruit picked. A yard that stops paying at night is a yard animals cross rather than settle in, and dens follow food more faithfully than any other signal. Does prevention work for bats as well as raccoons?

Yes, with bat-sized attention. Bats use gaps along ridge lines, gable ends, chimney flashing and the slots behind fascia that a raccoon ignores, so a prevention pass for bats works to a smaller gauge and a longer list of thin joints. The timing rule also applies: sealing a house with a colony in residence waits for the lawful windows. Prevention fitted to an empty house in September is exactly what stops next summer's colony choosing your ridge. That approach keeps the work humane, avoids sealing an animal inside, and gives you a record of exactly what was done.

What should I do first if I can only do a few things this weekend?

Cap or check the chimney, screen the gable and bathroom vents, lock the bins and bring pet food in, and write the roofline list even if the fixes wait. Those four moves close the two biggest entries, cut the food signal and turn the rest into a plan instead of a worry. Then book or diary the September walk. Prevention is a direction, and a weekend spent facing the right way beats a month of good intentions.
