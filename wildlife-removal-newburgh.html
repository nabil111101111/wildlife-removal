The animals Newburgh homeowners call about most

Raccoons in attics of older homes

Newburgh's older housing stock has plenty of soft spots : rotted trim, lifted flashing, open chimneys. Raccoons exploit them all, denning in attics and wall voids, and in row housing they can travel between connected attic spaces.

Opossums in basements and crawl spaces

Opossums slip into Newburgh basements and crawl spaces through broken foundation vents and coal chutes in older buildings. They nest quietly, leave droppings, and draw in other pests.

Skunks under porches and stoops

The raised porches and stoops of Newburgh's older homes are prime skunk dens. In dense neighborhoods, a skunk under one porch is a problem for every dog on the block.

Squirrels in soffits and wall voids

Street trees and utility lines give squirrels direct roof access across Newburgh. They chew into soffits and nest in walls, where gnawed wiring is a leading hidden hazard.

Signs an animal is already inside

  • Droppings below a roofline gap or in one attic corner
  • Rub marks beside a hole or vent
  • Flattened insulation with nesting material in piles
  • Chewed fascia, soffit corners or gable vent screens
  • Urine or musk smell on warm afternoons
  • Daylight visible through the roofline from the attic

The four step process, in the right order

Order matters. Seal too early and an animal can be trapped in a wall. Skip the seal and the next animal uses the same hole.

1. Inspection

Attic, roofline and foundation checked, entry points photographed. Written scope and price first.

2. Humane removal

One-way doors let animals leave and not return; ground animals are live caught. No poison.

3. Exclusion and sealing

Every gap closed with chew-proof metal, mesh or flashing. Chimneys capped, vents screened.

4. Cleanup

Droppings come out, the space disinfected, damaged insulation flagged.

Do you use poison for animals in the attic in Newburgh?

No poison is used for attic wildlife in Newburgh. Poisoned animals die in wall voids, where they smell for weeks and draw flies. Humane eviction with one-way doors, followed by sealing, removes the animal and closes the route it used to get in. Ask any operator who suggests poison how a dead animal leaves the wall.

Will you seal the entry points in my Newburgh home, or just remove the animal?

Entry points are sealed on every job in Newburgh, because removal alone does not last. The animal used a gap at the roofline, a vent or the foundation, and the next animal will find it. Sealing with chew-proof materials ends the cycle. Ask for the seal list in writing before work starts.

New York seasons, blackout dates, and timing in Newburgh

Timing is law for bats in New York: exclusion pauses in two blackout windows, May 1 to August 15, when flightless pups are in the roost, and October 1 to March 31, when colonies hibernate. Raccoon kits arrive March to June, so spring jobs start with a young check.

Can bats be removed from a Newburgh home during maternity season?

Bats cannot be excluded in Newburgh during the New York blackout windows, May 1 to August 15 and October 1 to March 31. Flightless pups cannot leave, and sealing them in is cruel and against state guidance. Inspections still run, and exclusion is booked for the day the window opens.

Prevention that holds in Orange County

  • Fit a stainless chimney cap
  • Screen vents with quarter inch hardware cloth
  • Replace soft fascia and rotted trim
  • Screen under decks and porches with buried mesh
  • Cut branches back six to eight feet from the roofline
  • Store pet food and bird seed in sealed metal bins
