What is active around Saratoga Springs, month by month

  • Jan: Deep winter. Observation records sit at their lowest, which mostly means the observers are indoors too. Bats are hibernating in place, mice are established in attics, and skunks den up through cold snaps.
  • Feb: Skunk mating season: males roam, and dog encounters at dusk rise. Groundhogs are still underground. Late-winter thaws bring the first raccoon den scouting along rooflines.
  • Mar: Raccoon kits begin arriving late in the month. Groundhogs emerge hungry. Starlings start probing vent runs, and mice are still travelling wall voids nightly.
  • Apr: Kit season in full swing. Squirrels raise spring litters in attics, groundhogs graze openly, and chimney swifts begin arriving at the end of the month.
  • May: Bat maternity season opens: from May 1, excluding an occupied bat colony is off the table in New York. Skunk kits are born under porches. Observation counts climb steeply as everything becomes visible.
  • Jun: The loud attic month: raccoon kits are mobile and noisy, squirrel litters are active at dawn, and swift nestlings keep occupied chimneys chirping through the day.
  • Jul: Young animals disperse and test new gaps. Bats are raising pups and cannot be excluded. Groundhogs feed hard and garden damage peaks.
  • Aug: The turning month. Swift young fledge and colonies leave by early autumn; bat pups are flying, so the exclusion window reopens after August 15. Post-season nest removal season begins.
  • Sep: Young are independent, colonies are leaving, and mild weather still allows ladder work. The records stay busy because everything is moving, fattening or dispersing.
  • Oct: The entry push opens: every species on this page is looking for winter shelter. Mice move indoors with the first cold nights. The second bat blackout begins October 1 for colonies hibernating in place.
  • Nov: Groundhogs are underground for the winter. Squirrels cache food and test rooflines, raccoons den up on cold nights, and attic mouse populations build quietly.
  • Dec: The winter pattern is set and observation records bottom out. Attic tenants are settled in; the work is inspections, the odd emergency, and planning spring sealing.

The two hard rules on this calendar

Bat exclusion blackouts

New York bat work stops twice a year. From May 1 to August 15, maternity colonies are raising flightless pups and exclusion is off the table. From October 1 to March 31, colonies in place are hibernating and are likewise left alone. Everything bat-shaped in Saratoga Springs is scheduled in the two windows between those dates, which is why the calendar pushes inspections in spring and sealing in September.

Babies change the plan, March to June

Raccoon kits, squirrel litters and skunk kits all arrive in the same quarter. Any eviction in that window starts with a young check, and sealing waits until the family can move together. The autumn entry push, October to March, is the mirror image: no young to protect, but every animal in Saratoga County is looking for a way in, which makes it the worst season to leave a known gap open.

Saratoga Springs wildlife calendar: questions and answers

What is the best month to seal my Saratoga Springs home against wildlife?

September, with October as the runner-up. Young animals are independent, bat and swift colonies have left or are leaving, the weather still allows ladder work, and the sealing lands just before the October to March entry push. February is the smart month for chimney caps specifically: fitted before the swifts return, with no timing questions at all.

Why do wildlife observations near Saratoga Springs peak in May?

Two reasons stacked together. The animals genuinely are most active and visible then: young are out, litters are mobile, and everything is feeding in the open. And the observers are out too: iNaturalist records rise when people are outside to make them. Read the calendar's low winter numbers the same way, as partly a recording effect. The animals do not leave Saratoga County in January; they just stop being watched.

Can bats be removed from my Saratoga Springs attic in June?

No. June sits inside the New York bat maternity blackout, May 1 to August 15, when flightless pups are in the roost and exclusion would trap them inside to die against the screens. The legal, working windows are early spring before the colony settles, and late summer into autumn once the pups are flying. A June call is still worth making: the inspection maps the entries so the August job takes one visit.

When do raccoons have babies in Saratoga Springs?

Kits are born from March through June, with the peak in April and May. They stay in the den for roughly eight weeks, which is why spring attic work starts with a young check and why sealing a den in April is the one move nobody should make. If you hear chattering or kit cries in a Saratoga Springs attic, that is a family, and the removal plan has to move the family together.

When do mice move into houses in Saratoga Springs?

With the first cold nights of autumn. Mice begin testing foundation gaps and garage door corners as overnight temperatures fall, and attic populations build through the winter. Average January temperatures near Saratoga Springs run about 26 degrees F, so by mid-winter the indoor population is the established one. Sealing at the quarter-inch standard is best done in late summer, before the push, rather than in response to it.

Is winter a bad time to call about a wildlife problem?

No. Winter calls are inspections, emergency removals of animals in living space, dead-animal work, and planning. What winter cannot always do is complete certain jobs: a hibernating groundhog's burrow cannot be proven empty, and a bat colony in place sits inside the October to March blackout. A winter inspection in Saratoga Springs still produces the entry list and the estimate, so spring work starts on day one.

How was this Saratoga Springs calendar put together?

From records, not folklore. The monthly numbers are research-grade iNaturalist observations within 25 km of Saratoga Springs, split into mammals and birds, fetched from the iNaturalist API. The homeowner actions are set by the New York rules that govern the work itself: the bat exclusion blackouts of May 1 to August 15 and October 1 to March 31, the March to June raccoon kit season, and the October to March entry push that every operator in Saratoga County plans around.
