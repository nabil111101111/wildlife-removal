Pets and Wildlife: Skunk Spray, Raccoons and Backyard Safety

Most pet and wildlife collisions happen at dusk in your own yard: a dog finds the skunk, a cat meets the raccoon, a raccoon finds the coop. This page covers the famous remedy, the real risks, and the yard habits that keep pets and wildlife in separate lanes.

The skunk remedy that actually works

When a dog is sprayed, the mixture with the strongest record is the home remedy credited to chemist Paul Krebaum: a quart of standard 3 percent hydrogen peroxide, a quarter cup of baking soda, and a teaspoon of liquid soap, mixed fresh in an open container and worked through the wet coat, avoiding the eyes, then rinsed. Use it promptly, because it works by changing the spray's chemistry on contact rather than masking it. Never store the mixed solution in a sealed bottle; it builds pressure. Tomato juice, by contrast, mostly tires out your nose while the spray stays on the dog.

Practical notes from hard experience: wash the dog outside if the weather allows, with gloves and old clothes, check the eyes and rinse them with clean water if the spray landed near the face, and expect the collar to hold the smell after the dog is clean, so wash or replace it. A dog sprayed at close range in the face, drooling or rubbing its eyes hard, deserves a call to your veterinarian. Then solve the cause: a skunk denning under the porch or crossing the yard nightly is a booking, not a monthly bath subscription.

Raccoons, dogs and the rest of the yard cast

A raccoon cornered by a dog can injure it badly; raccoons are strong, and a fight at a fence corner is the common pattern. Call dogs in at dusk where raccoons are active, and never let a dog pursue one under a deck. Groundhogs bite when a dog digs at the burrow entrance. Opossums are the exception in the cast: they rarely fight, play dead instead, and are more sinned against than sinning, though any wild animal bite to a pet means a prompt veterinarian call, with rabies the question the vet will work through with you and, where needed, health guidance.

Pet food is the yard's wildlife magnet, outranking every natural food in October. Feed indoors or clear bowls by dusk, store feed in sealed hard containers in the garage, and clean the grill tray. Outdoor cats face the wider cast list: raccoons at food bowls, coyotes and fishers beyond the yard in much of upstate New York, and the diseases and parasites that pass at shared feeding spots. A cat kept in from dusk removes most of the list at a stroke, and a wildlife-proof feeding routine removes the rest of the invitation.

Coops, gardens and the rural edge

Several of the pilot towns shade into farmland, and the same operators hear the coop version of the attic problem: raccoons test latches with their hands and take chickens through gaps a fox would not use. The defence is hardware, not hope: quarter-inch hardware cloth instead of chicken wire on openings, latches a raccoon's fingers cannot work, a buried mesh apron at the run, and birds locked in before dusk. Gardens answer to the groundhog page: fencing buried with an outward footer and the top left loose so it folds back on a climber. Pet-safe deterrents only, always: anything poisonous in a garden endangers the pets this page exists to protect, on top of the wildlife it aims at.

General safety rules that apply on every page here

Keep distance from any wild animal, even one that looks calm or injured. Keep children and pets away from droppings, nesting material and carcasses. Do not handle bats, raccoons or skunks with bare hands, and wash hands and tools after any contact with attic or crawlspace material. Photograph from a safe distance if you need a record. Where this page mentions health topics, it gives general information only, drawn from public health guidance, and it points you to health professionals and your county health department for advice about your own situation.

When to stop and get help first

Some situations should not wait for a routine appointment. A bat found in a room with a sleeping person, a child or anyone who cannot say whether they were bitten needs health guidance the same day. An animal acting strangely, staggering or approaching people in daylight should be reported, not approached. A carcass in a living space, or droppings across a large attic area, calls for professional cleanup rather than a household vacuum. Knowing which bucket your problem sits in is most of staying safe.

Frequently asked questions about pets and wildlife

What should I do if my dog gets sprayed by a skunk?

Keep the dog outside while you mix the remedy: a quart of 3 percent hydrogen peroxide, a quarter cup of baking soda and a teaspoon of liquid soap, made fresh in an open container. Wet the coat, work the mix through while avoiding the eyes, leave it a few minutes and rinse well. Gloves and old clothes for you, and wash or replace the collar afterwards. If the spray hit the eyes or the dog is drooling and distressed, call your veterinarian. How do I get skunk smell off a dog that tomato juice did not fix?

Tomato juice mainly fatigues your sense of smell; the spray oils stay in the coat. The peroxide, baking soda and soap mixture works on the chemistry instead and is the remedy groomers and veterinarians pass on. Use it fresh, never store it sealed, and repeat on the spots that took the direct hit. Thick double coats need the mix worked to the skin and more rinsing time. A commercial deskunk shampoo is the backup, used by the same thorough method. Will a raccoon attack my dog?

A raccoon would rather leave, and almost every fight starts with the dog cornering it. Given no exit, a raccoon fights hard and can wound a dog seriously, especially around the face and legs. Keep dogs leashed or called in at dusk where raccoons are active, never let a dog follow one under a deck, and treat any bite as a veterinarian call the same day. The prevention side is the same as ever: no food out, bins locked, dens excluded. Are opossums dangerous to dogs?

Rarely. Opossums avoid fights, bare their teeth as theatre and play dead when pressed, and they are not a significant rabies carrier compared with raccoons, skunks and bats. A curious dog may still get bitten by a cornered one, and any wild animal bite means a veterinarian call. In practice the opossum under your deck is eating ticks and snails and would like to be left to it; eviction, where needed, follows the standard one-way door method. Why does pet food outside attract wildlife?

Because it is the richest, most reliable food in the neighbourhood, restocked nightly at the same hour in the same bowl. Raccoons plan routes around it, skunks dig the lawn beside it and opossums commute to it. Feeding indoors, or clearing every bowl by dusk and storing feed in sealed containers, removes the signal within a week. It is the single highest-value change on this page, and it also ends the fights that happen at the bowl. My dog cornered a groundhog. What should I check?

Call the dog off and bring it in, then check for bites around the face, ears and legs once it is calm. Groundhogs bite when dug at or cornered, and any bite warrants a call to your veterinarian the same day, who will handle wounds and the vaccination questions. Check the burrow in daylight: if it sits under a shed or deck, the eviction and barrier sequence on the under-deck page is the fix, and keep the dog leashed in that part of the yard until it is done. That approach keeps the work humane, avoids sealing an animal inside, and gives you a record of exactly what was done.

How do I protect backyard chickens from raccoons?

Build for hands, not just teeth. Replace chicken wire on openings with quarter-inch hardware cloth, fit latches that need two motions to open, add a buried mesh apron around the run, and lock birds in before dusk without exception. Raccoons reach through wide mesh and pull birds to it, which is the grim detail that makes the cloth gauge matter. The groundhog and exclusion pages carry the barrier method that the same apron uses. Is it safe to let my cat roam where wildlife is active?

Roaming cats meet the full cast: raccoons at bowls, skunks at dusk, and beyond the yard in much of upstate New York, coyotes and fishers, for whom a cat is prey. Add the parasites and diseases shared at feeding spots and latrines. Keeping cats in from dusk removes most of the risk at once; full indoor life removes the rest. If your cat roams, keep vaccinations current through your veterinarian and bring the cat in the moment any wildlife problem is active at your address. Can pets catch anything from raccoon droppings in the yard?

Raccoon latrines can contain roundworm eggs that become infective in the environment, and dogs that eat or mouth contaminated soil or droppings are the usual route of concern. Keep dogs from latrine spots, which are often at the base of a tree, on a woodpile or by a den, clear droppings with gloves rather than letting the dog investigate, and raise any worry with your veterinarian, who can advise on your dog's case.
