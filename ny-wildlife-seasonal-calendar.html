New York Wildlife Seasonal Calendar

Wildlife work in New York runs on a calendar whether you plan by it or not. Bats cannot be excluded during two blackout windows, raccoon kits fill attics from March, and almost everything tries to move indoors between October and March. This page puts the year on one table.

The two bat windows that rule the diary

New York bat work is built around the bats' year. From May 1 to August 15, maternity season, colonies raise pups that cannot fly for their first weeks. Excluding then would seal flightless young inside to die and leave the colony's adults battering the seals. From October 1 to March 31, bats hibernate, many inside structures, and exclusion devices fitted to torpid bats fail the same way. Between those windows sit the working seasons: mid-August to the end of September, and April, once hibernation ends and before pups arrive. A colony found inside a blackout is inspected, made safe where the house allows, and booked for the first lawful window, not argued into an exception.

Those dates explain a pattern homeowners misread every summer: an operator who refuses a June bat job is not being difficult. That operator is following the only calendar that keeps the job legal and the colony alive, and the refusal, with the dates named, is a trust signal this site teaches on its verification page. Put the reverse in your notebook too. A firm eager to exclude bats in July, with no mention of pups, has told you how it treats rules you cannot see, in an attic you cannot enter.

  • Maternity blackout: May 1 to August 15
  • Hibernation blackout: October 1 to March 31
  • Working windows: April, and mid-August to September
  • Colonies found in a blackout are booked, not forced

Babies: the seasons that change every job

Raccoon kits run March to June, with mothers denning in attics and chimneys and young first heard as chattering and scuffling above ceilings. Squirrels run two litters, December to February and May to June, which is why squirrel noise can mean young in midwinter when nothing else is breeding. Skunk kits arrive in spring under porches and decks, groundhog young in April and May below sheds. Every one of these seasons switches jobs to the mother-and-babies protocol on the humane standard page: find the litter, age it, evict the family together, seal only on confirmed empty.

Outside the house, the calendar runs on animal movement. Skunks mate in February and March, when roaming males raise the odds of a sprayed dog. Groundhogs emerge as the ground thaws and start digging within days. Mice and rats press on houses hardest at the first cold snap, following warm air leaks through gaps of a quarter inch. The table below lays all of it out month by month, which is the form worth printing for the kitchen wall if your house sits near woods, water or a stone foundation.

A note on sources and limits

Season dates, licence rules and health notes on this site follow New York State Department of Environmental Conservation and New York State Department of Health public guidance as generally published. Rules and dates can change, and local county health departments sometimes add their own advice. Where a page touches health or law, it says so plainly and points you to the official source. Nothing here replaces an inspection of your house, advice from a health professional, or the current text of a regulation.

Frequently asked questions about the seasonal calendar

When is bat season in New York?

Bats are active in New York from spring to autumn, roosting in attics through the warm months and hibernating through winter, often in structures. The dates that matter for work are the blackouts: no exclusion from May 1 to August 15 for maternity, or October 1 to March 31 for hibernation. Activity you can see, dusk flights from a gable or guano building under a roost, runs roughly April to October in most years. An inspection in one of the ten upstate towns we cover confirms the details before any work or price is agreed in writing.

What is the best time of year for bat exclusion in New York?

Mid-August to the end of September is the prime window, once pups can fly and before hibernation starts, with April as the second window after hibernation and before maternity. Those weeks fill first in operator diaries across the state, so a colony found in summer should be inspected and booked early for August rather than left until the window is crowded. The exclusion itself then runs with the colony flying strongly and leaving on the first nights. If you are unsure, start with an inspection and written findings, then decide on the next step with clear information in hand.

When is raccoon baby season in New York?

Raccoon kits are born mainly from March to June, and mothers choose attics and chimneys for the den. Noise changes in those months: chattering, chirring and clumsy movement join the adult thumps. Any raccoon job in that window starts with a litter check, and removal follows the mother-and-babies protocol so the whole family leaves together. Sealing a den in April without that check is the commonest serious mistake in this trade. That approach keeps the work humane, avoids sealing an animal inside, and gives you a record of exactly what was done.

When do animals get into attics in New York?

The big push runs from October to March, as cold drives raccoons, squirrels, flying squirrels, mice and bats toward heated structures. Entry also spikes after storms that lift flashing and drop limbs, and in spring when mothers hunt den sites. September is the counter-move: a sealing pass over the roofline before the push, while the weather still allows ladder work, is the cheapest wildlife decision a New York homeowner makes all year. When is skunk mating season, and why does it matter?

Skunks mate in February and March. Males roam widely and fight, scent is common on still nights, and dogs meet skunks at their busiest and boldest. Females then den under porches and decks to raise kits through spring. For homeowners the season means two things: keep dogs leashed at dusk in late winter, and expect under-deck dens from March, which need the patient eviction timing on the animals-under-deck page rather than a rushed seal. When do groundhogs come out in New York?

Groundhogs emerge as the ground thaws, usually March into April in upstate New York, hungry and ready to dig. Burrows under sheds and decks are refreshed within days, and young arrive in April and May. The folklore date in February is a fairytale; soil temperature runs the real calendar. A burrow found active in spring should be evicted with the young in mind, then barred with the buried mesh barrier before the digging spreads toward footings. That approach keeps the work humane, avoids sealing an animal inside, and gives you a record of exactly what was done.

What are the squirrel breeding seasons?

Grey squirrels raise two litters a year in New York: December to February and May to June. The winter litter surprises homeowners who assume breeding is a spring affair, and it changes January squirrel jobs: young may be in the nest while snow is on the roof. Flying squirrels breed in spring and summer and group together for winter warmth. Any squirrel eviction starts with a nest check for exactly this reason, whatever the month. Why do I hear more attic noise in winter?

Because winter is occupancy season. The animals that entered during the October to March push are settled above heated rooms, moving through insulation on nightly rounds, and in December the first squirrel litters add young to the sound. Cold also opens fresh gaps as timber and flashing contract, so new tenants join old ones. Winter noise, logged with times, identifies most species from the pattern alone using the diagnostic guide on this site. Is there a deadline for sealing my house before winter?

Treat the first hard freeze as the soft deadline and September as the working one. General exclusion, vents, soffits, caps and screens, can be fitted any time the structure is empty and the weather allows safe ladder work, but autumn diaries fill as the entry push begins and every homeowner calls at once. Bat work has its own hard edges, the September 30 close of the main window, after which colonies wait for spring.
