About This Service: How Matching Works

Plainly stated: this site is a matching service, not a crew with a truck. Homeowners in ten upstate towns describe a wildlife problem, and we connect them with a licensed local operator who does the work. This page explains why the service is built that way and what standards sit behind it.

What this pilot will not do

Three refusals shape the service as much as its promises. It will not publish a review, rating or testimonial that did not come from a real, named job, even when the page looks emptier for it. It will not invent a local office, a crew photograph or a year of trading that does not exist. And it will not send an operator to a job who cannot be verified against the licence standard on this site. Those refusals cost the site some decoration. They buy the one asset this trade is shortest on, which is a homeowner's justified belief that the next sentence is also true. Everything else here, the town research, the price ranges, the seasonal dates, is checked against sources for the same reason. If you catch an error, the contact route on this page reaches a person who would rather fix it than defend it.

Why a matching service at all

Upstate wildlife work is local by nature. The operator who knows Ithaca gorges and Cayuga Lake cottages is not the one who knows Watertown snow-belt roofs, and a homeowner in Elmira gains nothing from a call centre two states away. Matching keeps the work with operators who actually cover the town, know its housing stock and can be back for a warranty check without a day of travel. It also lets this site do one job well: clear information, honest ranges and a standard worth holding operators to.

Some websites in this trade hide what they are. Dispatch networks show a town name on every page in the state, use stock photographs of people who do not work there, and in the worst case found in our own competitor research, publish testimonials the site itself admits are illustrative, performed by actors. We take the opposite bet: say plainly that this is a matching service, name the standard, publish real price ranges, and let trust do the ranking work that tricks do elsewhere.

  • This site matches, it does not pretend to be the crew
  • Partner operators are named once signed, never invented
  • No reviews are published until real ones exist
  • Price ranges are published, not hidden behind a form

Why we built this

The pilot began with a simple comparison. In these ten towns, the pages ranking for wildlife help were thin, copied from town to town, or published by firms with no presence here at all, including pages written for other states appearing under New York searches. Homeowners at 2 a.m. with a raccoon over the bedroom deserved better information than that. So the site was built town by town, with local detail checked per town, real seasonal dates for New York, and a diagnostic guide that answers the first question, what is up there, before asking for a phone number.

The pilot runs on a test-first basis across two trades, and this wildlife side covers Ithaca, Binghamton, Poughkeepsie, Kingston, Newburgh, Utica, Saratoga Springs, Watertown, Auburn and Elmira. If the information here earns its keep, the model extends. If a page here is wrong, the standard we publish on the verification page is the stick to beat us with, and we would rather be corrected than copied from a network template.

The humane standard behind every match

Operators matched here work to the humane standard set out on its own page: no poison, one-way doors and lawful trapping where needed, a mother-and-babies protocol in season, exclusion with metal materials, and cleanup that treats droppings with the care they need. Warranty terms are the operator's own and are given to you in writing before work starts. If an operator ever falls short of the standard on a matched job, that is information this service wants to hear, because the standard is the product.

The standard we hold operators to

Every operator matched through this site is expected to hold a current New York nuisance wildlife control operator licence, carry insurance, work humanely without poison, and put findings and prices in writing before work starts. Those are not marketing lines. They are checks a homeowner can repeat in minutes using the steps on this site, and they are the same checks we suggest you run on any company you find elsewhere, including firms a neighbour recommends. A good operator welcomes the questions. An operator who dodges them has told you something useful about how the job will go.

Written findings beat verbal promises

Wildlife work happens in attics, on roofs and under decks, in places most homeowners never see. That makes paperwork matter more here than in most trades. Ask for photographs of entry points before and after sealing, a list of materials used, notes on any young animals found and how they were handled, and the terms of any warranty in writing. Keep the file with your house records. It helps with insurance questions, with a future sale, and with the next operator if a different species turns up two winters from now.

Frequently asked questions about this service

Are your wildlife removal methods humane?

Yes, and humane here has a working definition, not just a warm sound. Animals are evicted with one-way doors or trapped lawfully where doors cannot work, mothers are kept with their young, poison is never used, and entry points are sealed so the animal can live elsewhere rather than die in your wall. The full standard is published on the humane removal page, so any matched operator can be held to it line by line. An inspection in one of the ten upstate towns we cover confirms the details before any work or price is agreed in writing.

What areas do you serve?

The pilot covers ten upstate New York towns: Ithaca, Binghamton, Poughkeepsie, Kingston, Newburgh, Utica, Saratoga Springs, Watertown, Auburn and Elmira, with the homes around them. Each town has its own page with local detail, because roofing, housing age and animal pressure differ across the region. If your home sits just outside a listed town, ask anyway. Operators work by territory, not by town sign, and the nearest match may still cover you. If you are unsure, start with an inspection and written findings, then decide on the next step with clear information in hand.

Do you offer free assessments?

Inspections on this site run $150-$300 and are often credited toward removal work by the operator, which is the honest version of the free-assessment promise. A real inspection involves a roofline walk, attic time and photographs, and a professional's hour. Services that advertise free everything usually recover the cost inside the job price. Ask any operator, matched or not, whether the inspection fee is credited, and get the answer with the quote. That approach keeps the work humane, avoids sealing an animal inside, and gives you a record of exactly what was done.

Do you use pesticides for wildlife work?

No. This service covers wildlife: raccoons, bats, squirrels, skunks, groundhogs, opossums, mice and rats in structures, and birds in chimneys and vents. It does not spray for insects and it does not sell pest subscriptions. Poison for wildlife is refused outright, for the reasons set out on the humane standard page. If your problem turns out to be insects, a pest control firm is the right call and we will say so. An inspection in one of the ten upstate towns we cover confirms the details before any work or price is agreed in writing.

Do you warranty your work?

Exclusion work carries the matched operator's own written warranty on the points sealed, and the terms are part of your quote before work starts. Those terms name the points, the period and the remedy if an animal re-enters through a sealed point. This service does not publish a blanket warranty of its own, because a promise is only worth the operator standing behind it, and that operator is named on your paperwork. If you are unsure, start with an inspection and written findings, then decide on the next step with clear information in hand.

How is this different from a pest control company?

The work differs in kind. Pest control manages insects and routine rodent pressure, often on a subscription. Wildlife work is a building job with an animal in it: roofline inspection, humane eviction, metal exclusion, cleanup and restoration. The firms overlap on mice and rats and diverge on everything larger. A specialist wildlife operator also works under the New York nuisance wildlife licence described on the verification page, which general pest licensing does not replace. That approach keeps the work humane, avoids sealing an animal inside, and gives you a record of exactly what was done.

Who actually comes to my house?

A licensed nuisance wildlife control operator whose territory covers your town, matched to your request. At launch, partner details are confirmed on your booking paperwork, and the verification page on this site shows you how to check the licence yourself with the Department of Environmental Conservation. Nobody is sent who cannot be checked. That is a deliberate contrast with dispatch sites that will not name who is coming at all. An inspection in one of the ten upstate towns we cover confirms the details before any work or price is agreed in writing.

Why does the site publish prices when others do not?

Because the price question is the first question every homeowner asks, and hiding it serves the seller, not the buyer. The ranges here are typical market ranges, stated once and used across the whole site, so a quote can be compared against them line by line. An operator whose price lands outside the ranges should be able to explain why in terms of access, entry points or contamination. That conversation is easier for everyone when the ranges were never a secret.
