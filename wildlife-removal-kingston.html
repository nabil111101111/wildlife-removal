The animals Kingston homeowners call about most

Bats in historic attics

Kingston's pre-1900 homes often have ridge gaps, loose slate or shingles and unsealed chimney flashing : a bat highway into big, quiet attics. Colonies can live above Stockade and Uptown homes for years before discovery.

Squirrels in eaves of older frame homes

Squirrels are relentless in Kingston's tree-lined neighborhoods, chewing into eaves and soffits of older frame houses and nesting in wall cavities. In historic homes, the resulting gnaw damage is expensive to repair.

Raccoons near Rondout Creek

The creek corridor and wooded edges of Kingston feed a healthy raccoon population. They den in chimneys and under porches, and raid the trash of homes backing onto green space.

Skunks and opossums under outbuildings

Kingston's carriage houses, barns and sheds give skunks and opossums sheltered den sites. Opossums in particular take up residence under outbuildings and in crawl spaces along the creek.

Signs an animal is already inside

  • Droppings below a roofline gap or in one attic corner
  • Rub marks beside a hole or vent
  • Flattened insulation with nesting material in piles
  • Chewed fascia, soffit corners or gable vent screens
  • Urine or musk smell on warm afternoons
  • Daylight visible through the roofline from the attic

The four step process, in the right order

Order matters. Seal too early and an animal can be trapped in a wall. Skip the seal and the next animal uses the same hole.

1. Inspection

Attic, roofline and foundation checked, entry points photographed. Written scope and price first.

2. Humane removal

One-way doors let animals leave and not return; ground animals are live caught. No poison.

3. Exclusion and sealing

Every gap closed with chew-proof metal, mesh or flashing. Chimneys capped, vents screened.

4. Cleanup

Droppings come out, the space disinfected, damaged insulation flagged.

Do you use poison for animals in the attic in Kingston?

Prevention that holds in Ulster County

  • Fit a stainless chimney cap
  • Screen vents with quarter inch hardware cloth
  • Replace soft fascia and rotted trim
  • Screen under decks and porches with buried mesh
  • Cut branches back six to eight feet from the roofline
  • Store pet food and bird seed in sealed metal bins
